CoolSynth · V3 engine

A synthesizer built from slots

CoolSynth is a polyphonic software synthesizer, a VST3 plugin and a standalone app, written in C++ with JUCE. Its engine is made of slots: any sound source in any of three generator slots, any filter or shaper in any of three processor slots, any effect in any effect slot, all of it modulated from one matrix.

Work in progress: the engine still changes week to week, and the instrument's own interface comes last.

A patch

Two parts, each a complete synth with its own voices, modulation and effects, played alone, layered or split across the keyboard. They share sixteen voices: each is sure of eight and gets more when the other needs fewer.

Each voice runs three generator slots into three processor slots in series and an amplifier, at twice the host's sample rate. Play modes: poly, mono, legato and unison, with glide by time or by rate.

Modulation

Per voice: three envelopes with hold, curves and key-scaled times, two LFOs, velocity, key, note random and drift. Per part: two global LFOs, a looping global envelope, a 16-step sequencer with slides, four macros and a chaos source.

A 24-slot matrix routes any of them to pitch, level, pan, any slot's values and the envelope times; each routing can be scaled by a second source and bent by a curve.

Notes in

Chord memory (twelve chord shapes, or one learned from the keys), strum, and an arpeggiator with eleven patterns, swing, ratchets, accents and Euclidean rhythms, all before the parts.

Effects: three slots on each part, then six master slots, each holding any of twelve effects in any order. What each slot can hold, generator, processor or effect, is listed under the map.

Sounds, and how they are checked

118 factory presets in 13 rooms. The Conservatory holds 40 recorded acoustic instruments from free (CC0) sample libraries, played through the Sample generator. The Museum holds classic synth sounds rebuilt from the instruments' own patch data, or from a record when no data exists, and each is admitted only after a side-by-side listening test against a recording of the real thing.

Every module has unit tests, and sounds are measured against recordings (harmonic levels, band levels, envelopes, pitch) before they are judged by ear.
